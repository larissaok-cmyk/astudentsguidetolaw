The Declaration of Independence (1776)

Article written by A Student's Guide to Law. Last updated: June 29, 2026

The Declaration of Independence basically formally created the United States as a whole. Explore the historical context and its effects on our society

What is now the United States of America had been previously colonized by Great Britain from 1607 to 1783, beginning with the colonization of Jamestown, Virginia and concluding with the end of the American Revolutionary War and the signing of the Treaty of Paris in 1783. British powers often took advantage of the American colonies in many ways, some examples include forcing the colonies to export raw materials from their territory to the mother country (Britain) while taxing a lot on the manufactured goods (the ones that came out of British factories) they were forced to buy back. Colonists were also prohibited from trading goods with other countries that were not Britain, therefore only enriching themselves. A document (The Declaration of Independence) was then created to declare the 13 colonies fully independent from British powers, unify them, and secure new foreign alliances. To do that, they needed to make the American colonies sovereign and capable of making treaties and waging war. This document was fueled by many Enlightenment principles, especially the ideas of popular sovereignty and the social contract. The document was written by Thomas Jefferson.
